The first image is the person. The second image is the garment reference.

Replace the person's lower-body garment with the garment from the reference
image. Match the reference exactly: same garment category, colour, pattern,
fabric texture, waistband, fly and closure, pockets, leg cut and total length.
Fit it naturally to the hips and legs with realistic folds and shadows
following the pose.

Change nothing above the waist. The upper-body clothing, its hem, its colour and
its drape stay exactly as in the first image, and so do the face, hair, hands,
footwear and background.

Match the reference garment's exact coverage and length. Generate realistic legs
only where they are newly exposed by the reference garment, with skin tone,
texture and lighting matching the person's visible skin. If the reference
garment is longer than the original clothing, extend it naturally down the legs
exactly as shown in the reference, with realistic folds, seams and fabric detail.

Waist and belt. Read the waistband off the reference before drawing it, and
build whichever of these two constructions it actually shows. A pull-on waist is
one smooth continuous band of the same cloth, softly gathered all the way round
and closed by a drawstring whose two ends hang at the centre front. A denim or
tailored waist is a flat firm band with a fly, a button or hook closure, and
belt loops spaced evenly around it. Draw one or the other and never parts of
both, and match the number and spacing of any loops. If the top covers the
waistband in the reference so that you cannot see how it is built, let the top
cover it the same way here and leave the waist plain, rather than inventing a
drawstring, a buckle or loops to fill the gap.

Pockets. Read the reference before drawing any pocket, and draw only the ones you
can actually see in it: the same number, in the same places, at the same size and
angle. A slanted or seamed pocket at the hip, and a welt or patch pocket at the
seat, appear only where the reference shows them. Where you cannot clearly make
out a pocket, there is none. The pocket layout comes from the reference alone —
not from the garment the person is wearing now, and not from what this kind of
garment usually carries.

The outside of each leg is smooth uninterrupted cloth from the hip down past the
knee to the hem, broken only by its own seams and folds. Nothing is applied,
patched or flapped onto the thigh unless the reference plainly shows a pocket
sitting there with its own visible outline and stitching, in which case it is
matched at the same height on both legs. A soft pull-on or drawstring garment in
particular runs plain down the outside of the leg. Where the reference thigh is
plain cloth, the finished thigh is plain cloth.
