The first image is the person. The second image is the garment reference.

Put the pants from the second image onto the person in the first. The second
image is the only authority for the lower-body garment: its colour, its cloth,
its cut, its length and every detail of how it is built. The first image is the
only authority for the person, the pose, the framing, the light and the
background, and it says nothing about how the new garment should be made.

The person's own trousers go completely. Their colour, their cut and their
length leave the picture, and no part of the old waistband, pocket, crease or
hem shows through the new cloth. The shirt above stays exactly as it is — same
colour, same length, same drape — falling over the new waistband as it falls
now. Nothing above the waist changes, and neither do the face, hair, hands,
footwear or background.

Coverage and length come from the reference. The hem falls exactly where the
reference puts it. Where the new garment is shorter than the old one, the newly
exposed leg is drawn as real skin, matching the person's own tone, texture and
lighting. Where it is longer, the cloth continues down the leg with its own
folds, seams and hem.

Cloth and colour. Take the colour from the reference and hold it: the same hue,
the same depth, the same saturation, and the same weave and surface — a smooth
cotton twill stays smooth, a soft brushed knit stays soft and matte, a ribbed or
textured cloth keeps its texture at the same scale. Do not carry over the colour
of the trousers the person is wearing now, and do not retune it to the light or
the background.

Waist. Read the waistband off the reference and build only the construction it
actually shows, never parts of two. If the band is flat and firm, build it flat
and firm: a smooth unbroken band of the same cloth fastened at the centre front
by a button or hook with a fly below it, with no gathering anywhere and no cord
at the front. If the band is soft and gathered into even vertical folds, build it
that way, with the two ends of its cord hanging at the centre front only where
the reference shows them and no fly. A flat band never carries a drawstring, and
a gathered band never carries a fly. Belt loops appear only where the reference
plainly shows them, matched in number, width and spacing; where the reference
band is clean, it stays clean all the way round. If the shirt covers the
waistband in the reference so that you cannot see how it closes, let it stay
covered here and leave the band plain rather than inventing a closure, a cord or
loops to fill the gap.

Pockets. Read the reference before drawing any pocket, and draw only the ones you
can actually see in it: the same number, in the same places, at the same size and
angle. A slanted or seamed pocket at the hip, and a welt or patch pocket at the
seat, appear only where the reference shows them. Where you cannot clearly make
out a pocket, there is none. The pocket layout comes from the reference alone —
not from the trousers the person is wearing now, and not from what this kind of
garment usually carries.

The outside of each leg is smooth uninterrupted cloth from the hip down past the
knee to the hem, broken only by its own seams and folds. Nothing is applied,
patched or flapped onto the thigh unless the reference plainly shows a pocket
sitting there with its own visible outline and stitching, in which case it is
matched at the same height on both legs. A soft pull-on or drawstring garment in
particular runs plain down the outside of the leg. Where the reference thigh is
plain cloth, the finished thigh is plain cloth.

Leg cut. Match the reference's rise and how wide the leg runs from hip to hem: a
wide leg stays wide, a straight leg stays straight, a tapered leg stays tapered.
Match how the leg finishes — a plain open hem, a turned cuff, or a gathered
elastic cuff at the ankle — and draw only the finish the reference shows. The legs
read as two separate legs, each in its own leg of cloth, with the gap between
them visible and the outline following each leg. Add realistic folds at the hip,
the knee and the hem, with shadows following the existing pose and light
direction.

Keep the person's face, hair, skin tone, body shape, hands, pose, footwear,
background, lighting and camera angle exactly as they are.
