The person is wearing a kurta over a shalwar. Add the jacket from the reference
image as a layer worn over the kurta.

This is layering, not replacement. The kurta stays on underneath and keeps its
exact colour, print and fabric. Because the jacket is shorter than the kurta,
the kurta continues below the jacket hem and remains clearly visible down to its
own hem, and it also shows at the neckline, along the front opening if the
jacket is open, and at the cuffs if the jacket sleeves are shorter. The jacket
sits on top with a slightly larger outer silhouette, so the shoulders and torso
read as two layers thick.

Before drawing, read each of these features off the jacket in the second image
and reproduce it exactly as it appears there. Do not substitute a generic jacket
for any of them, and do not simplify a detail away because it is small:

- Closure: zip, snaps, or buttons. If buttoned, count the rows and the buttons
  in each row and reproduce that same count and spacing. If zipped, reproduce

  same width and worn the same way.
- Cuffs and hem: plain, ribbed knit, elasticated, or with a buttoned tab or
  strap. Any cuff strap or button in the reference appears on both sleeves.
- Pockets: flap, welt, patch, slash or zipped chest, at the same height and
  angle.
- Hardware and stitching: the same zips, studs, rivets, epaulettes and visible
  topstitching, in the same finish and contrast as the reference.
- Colour: the same hue and saturation as the reference. Keep it as vivid as it
  is there; do not wash it toward grey to blend with the scene.
- Material: the same weave, grain or sheen — denim, leather, wool, nylon or
