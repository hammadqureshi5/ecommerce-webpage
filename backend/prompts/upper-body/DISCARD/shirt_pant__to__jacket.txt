This is layering, not replacement. The person's own shirt stays on underneath
and keeps its exact colour and pattern wherever it remains visible: at the
collar, along the front opening if the jacket is open, and at the cuffs if the
jacket sleeves are shorter. The jacket sits on top of it with a slightly larger
outer silhouette, so the shoulders read as two layers thick rather than one.

Before drawing, read each of these features off the jacket in the second image
and reproduce it exactly as it appears there. Do not substitute a generic jacket

- Closure: zip, snaps, or buttons. If buttoned, count the rows and the buttons

- Cuffs and hem: plain, ribbed knit, elasticated, or with a buttoned tab or
  strap, ending at the waist or hip exactly as in the reference. Any cuff strap

- Pockets: flap, welt, patch, slash or zipped chest, at the same height and
  angle.
- Hardware and stitching: the same zips, studs, rivets, epaulettes and visible
  topstitching, in the same finish and contrast as the reference.
- Colour: the same hue and saturation as the reference. Keep it as vivid as it
  is there; do not wash it toward grey to blend with the scene.
- Material: the same weave, grain or sheen — denim, leather, wool, nylon or
  knit — with the texture still legible at this size.
- Fit: the same cut, from cropped and fitted through relaxed and boxy.
